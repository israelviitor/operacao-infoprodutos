MATH MISSIONSLevel 3 · Mission 3-01For the adult · read first (2 minutes)

The Mystery of the Long Jump

Time: 25 to 35 minutes · Practice: decimal numbers in meters: adding and subtracting · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 3 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use a sewing tape measure or a retractable tape measure: your child measures 3.35 m on the floor and sees how much is missing to reach 4 m. In stage 3, they mark Sofia's three jumps one after the other before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 3 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 3 in their own words. If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., a total of 11 m, jumps of 3.85 m and 3.6 m).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Mystery of the Long Jump

Stage 1 — How much is missing to reach 4 m · 0.65 m (letter T)
How to think: Write 4 as 4.00 and subtract with the decimal points lined up: 4.00 − 3.35. Calculation: 4.00 − 3.35 = 0.65 m.
If they get stuck, ask: “From 3.35 to 3.40, how much is missing? And from 3.40 to 4.00?”
Most common mistake: answering 1.35 m, when your child subtracted only the 3 (4 − 3 = 1) and copied the 0.35 without subtracting. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — Rui's two jumps · 7.15 m (letter A)
How to think: Write 3.8 as 3.80 and add with the decimal points lined up. Calculation: 3.35 + 3.80 = 7.15 m.
If they get stuck, ask: “Is 3.8 the same as 3.80 or as 3.08?”
Most common mistake: answering 6.43 m, when your child read 3.8 as 3.08 and added without lining up the decimal points. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — Sofia's 3rd jump · 4.35 m (letter P)
How to think: The sum of the 3 jumps is the whole. Put the known parts together and take them away from the whole. Calculation: 3.95 + 3.70 = 7.65 · 12.00 − 7.65 = 4.35 m.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Part-whole with decimals and 3 parts: the two known parts add up to 7.65 m; the missing one is 12.00 − 7.65.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many parts do you already know? What do they add up to?”
Most common mistake: answering 8.05 m, when your child subtracted only the 1st jump and forgot about the 2nd. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The real winner · 0.55 m (letter E)
How to think: Compare Sofia's right measurement (4.35) with Rui's (3.80). Calculation: 4.35 − 3.80 = 0.55 m.
If they get stuck, ask: “Which of Sofia's measurements is the right one? Now compare it with 3.80.”
Most common mistake: answering 0.35 m, when your child used the wrong measurement that was written down (3.45) and thought Rui won. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: TAPE. The judge wrote down Sofia's 3rd jump as 3.45 m, but the measuring TAPE showed 4.35 m. With the right measurement, Sofia jumped 0.55 m more than Rui, and the medal went back to her.
