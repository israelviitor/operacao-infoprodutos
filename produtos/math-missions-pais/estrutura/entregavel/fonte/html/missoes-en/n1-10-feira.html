The Great Neighborhood Fair Mystery

Time: 25 to 35 minutes · Practice: Level 1 Final Mission: putting together, taking away and comparing, equal groups and half, with gold game coins · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

This is the level's Final Mission: all 4 stages are drawing stages, and each one uses one of the drawings your child has already mastered. In each stage, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Sort 80 bottle caps into 8 little piles of 10 (the gold coins). Use the piles to check stages 1, 3, and 4 before each drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Here all 4 stages are drawing stages: set aside more time and, if needed, split the mission over 2 days.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your 4 drawings to me.” To move up a level, the code word has to work out and your child has to explain at least 3 of the 4 drawings on their own.
Before moving on: This is the Level 1 Final Mission. Your child moves up to Level 2 when the code word works out and they explain at least 3 of the 4 drawings on their own. If not yet, redo just the drawings that were hard another day, with different numbers, and come back to this mission.

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Great Neighborhood Fair Mystery

Stage 1 — The day's sales · 80 (letter C)
How to think: Morning and afternoon are the parts; the whole day is the total: 35 + 45. Calculation: 35 + 45 = 80.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Part-whole with the ? on the whole: it's addition, 35 + 45.
If they get stuck, ask: “What is 5 + 5? Does that make one more ten?”
Most common mistake: answering 70, when your child added only the tens and forgot that 5 + 5 makes one more ten. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The juice stand · 18 (letter A)
How to think: Comparing is finding the piece the bigger bar has extra: 80 − 62. Calculation: 80 − 62 = 18.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Comparison: the dashed piece is the difference, 80 − 62.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many more does the juice stand need to reach 80?”
Most common mistake: answering 142, when your child added the two stands instead of comparing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.

Stage 3 — The little bags · 60 (letter K)
How to think: There are 6 equal groups of 10: count by 10s six times. Calculation: 6 × 10 = 60.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Equal pieces with the ? on the total: the math is 6 × 10.
If they get stuck, ask: “Count by 10s, once for each little bag.”
Most common mistake: answering 16, when your child added 6 + 10 instead of putting together 6 little bags of 10. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The missing coins · 10 (letter E)
How to think: The 20 missing coins are the whole. Half of that: 20 ÷ 2. Calculation: 20 ÷ 2 = 10.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Half is 1 of the 2 equal pieces: 20 ÷ 2.
If they get stuck, ask: “If 20 coins are divided into 2 equal little piles, how many are in each one?”
Most common mistake: answering 20, when your child answered with the 20 missing coins, without dividing in half. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: CAKE. The coins didn't disappear: half of the missing ones were inside an empty CAKE box, and the other half were in the vendor's apron. The cash box added up to 80!
