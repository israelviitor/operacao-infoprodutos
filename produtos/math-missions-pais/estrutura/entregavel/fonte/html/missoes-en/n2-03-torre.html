The Riddle of the Block Tower

Time: 25 to 30 minutes · Practice: multiplicative comparison: times as many · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use building blocks, small boxes, or books. In stage 2, your child builds a tower of 8 and, next to it, 3 towers of 8, and counts everything together before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“each of Davi's pieces is the size of Léo's tower”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 7 blocks, 4 times).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Riddle of the Block Tower

Stage 1 — The height of Léo's tower · 48 cm (letter D)
How to think: There are 8 equal blocks of 6 cm: 8 × 6. Calculation: 8 × 6 = 48 cm.
If they get stuck, ask: “How tall is 1 block? And 2? And 8?”
Most common mistake: answering 14 cm, when your child added 8 + 6 instead of putting together 8 blocks of 6 cm. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — 3 times Léo's tower · 32 (letter E)
How to think: “3 times” is 3 pieces equal to Léo's tower. Then, put the two towers together. Calculation: 3 × 8 = 24 (Davi) · 24 + 8 = 32.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
“3 times” becomes 3 pieces equal to Léo's. The two towers together are 4 pieces of 8: 32.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many blocks would Davi have? And the two towers together?”
Most common mistake: answering 24, when your child found only Davi's tower and forgot to put Léo's together with it. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — Davi's count · 8 (letter S)
How to think: From 16 to 24, what's missing is 24 − 16. Calculation: 24 − 16 = 8.
If they get stuck, ask: “If it has 16 and should have 24, how many are missing?”
Most common mistake: answering 40, when your child added instead of finding how many are missing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The secret of the height · 168 cm (letter K)
How to think: First, the height of the blocks (16 × 6). Then, add the desk. Calculation: 16 × 6 = 96 · 96 + 72 = 168 cm.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many centimeters tall is the tower by itself? And with the desk underneath?”
Most common mistake: answering 88 cm, when your child added the number of blocks, not their height. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: DESK. Davi's tower had only 16 blocks: double, not triple. It looked huge because it was on top of the DESK!
