The Case of the Shared Chalk

Time: 20 to 25 minutes · Practice: division: sharing equally · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use 12 pencils or sticks as chalk and 3 small plates, one for each grandchild. In stage 2, your child hands out one on each plate, one round at a time, before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“each piece is a grandchild”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 15 pieces of chalk for 3 grandchildren).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Case of the Shared Chalk

Stage 1 — The box of chalk · 12 (letter P)
How to think: There are 2 equal rows of 6: 6 + 6. Calculation: 2 × 6 = 12 (or 6 + 6).
If they get stuck, ask: “How many pieces of chalk are in one row? What about in two?”
Most common mistake: answering 8, when your child added 2 + 6 instead of putting together 2 rows of 6. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The sharing · 4 (letter I)
How to think: Sharing equally is making 3 parts of the same size: 12 ÷ 3. Calculation: 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The total is known and the ? is on a piece equal to the others: it's division, 12 ÷ 3.
If they get stuck, ask: “If each grandchild gets 1 piece of chalk per round, how many rounds can you do with 12?”
Most common mistake: answering 9, when your child took away 3 instead of sharing into 3 equal parts. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The pink chalk · 0 (letter N)
How to think: There were 3. Theo took away 2 and Lia took away 1. What was left is 3 − 2 − 1. Calculation: 3 − 2 − 1 = 0.
If they get stuck, ask: “Take 3 pencils: take away 2 for Theo and 1 for Lia. Is any left?”
Most common mistake: answering 6, when your child added all the numbers in the problem. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The fair trade · 1 (letter K)
How to think: Theo keeps 1 and gives away whatever is more than that: 2 − 1. Calculation: 2 − 1 = 1.
If they get stuck, ask: “If Theo gives away 2, how many does he have left? Is that fair?”
Most common mistake: answering 2, when your child gave away all of Theo's pink chalk, and he was left with none. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: PINK. Each grandchild got 4 pieces of chalk, but the 3 PINK pieces all ended up with Theo and Lia. Theo gave 1 to Bia, and the sharing became truly fair.
