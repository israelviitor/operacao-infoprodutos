The Mystery of the Swapped Lunchbox

Time: 20 to 25 minutes · Practice: addition: putting together (up to 100) · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Set aside dried beans or bottle caps to be the grapes. In stage 2, your child makes one little pile of 24 and another of 15, puts the two together, and counts before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“this part is…, this other part is…, and the total goes on top”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 32 grapes on top and 16 on the bottom).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Mystery of the Swapped Lunchbox

Stage 1 — Lunchbox A · 11 (letter M)
How to think: The green grapes and the purple grapes are together in the same lunchbox: 6 + 5. Calculation: 6 + 5 = 11.
If they get stuck, ask: “If you put the green grapes and the purple grapes on one plate, how many are there?”
Most common mistake: answering 1, when your child took away instead of putting together. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — Lunchbox B · 39 (letter O)
How to think: The two containers are parts of the same lunchbox. The total is the sum of the parts: 24 + 15. Calculation: 24 + 15 = 39.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The drawing shows 2 parts and the unknown total on top. When the ? is on the whole, it's addition: 24 + 15.
If they get stuck, ask: “What does the question want to know: the grapes in one container or in the whole lunchbox?”
Most common mistake: answering 9, when your child subtracted, without seeing that the two containers go together. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The napkins · 27 (letter R)
How to think: There are 3 groups: 10, 10, and 7. Put it all together: 10 + 10 = 20, plus 7. Calculation: 10 + 10 + 7 = 27.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many groups of napkins are there? Did you count all of them?”
Most common mistake: answering 20, when your child forgot the loose napkins. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — All the grapes · 50 (letter E)
How to think: Add the tens (10 + 30 = 40) and the ones (1 + 9 = 10). Then put them together: 40 + 10. Calculation: 11 + 39 = 50 (1 + 9 = 10; 10 + 30 + 10 = 50).
If they get stuck, ask: “What is 1 + 9? Does that make one more ten?”
Most common mistake: answering 40, when your child added only the tens and forgot that 1 + 9 makes one more ten. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: MORE. MORE grapes solved the case: lunchbox B, with 39 grapes, is Lia's. Lunchbox A, with 11, is Theo's. Each of them had lunch with their own!
