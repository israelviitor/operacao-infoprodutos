The Riddle of the Race Stopwatch

Time: 25 to 30 minutes · Practice: time in minutes and seconds; adding durations · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use the stopwatch on a phone: your child times how long it takes them to do one lap around the room. In stage 2, they put together 4 strips of paper, one for each lap, before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“each piece is one lap”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 85 s, 92 s, 88 s, and 95 s).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Riddle of the Race Stopwatch

Stage 1 — The seconds · 120 s (letter L)
How to think: Each minute has 60 seconds. Two minutes: 60 + 60. Calculation: 2 × 60 = 120 s.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many seconds are in 1 minute? And in 2?”
Most common mistake: answering 62 s, when your child added 2 instead of putting together 2 minutes of 60 seconds. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The 4 laps · 377 s (letter A)
How to think: The 4 laps are the parts of the race. Add one at a time. Calculation: 95 + 90 = 185 · 185 + 100 = 285 · 285 + 92 = 377 s.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Part-whole with time: the laps, one after the other, make up the race. The total is the sum of the 4 parts.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many laps were there? Did you add all 4?”
Most common mistake: answering 282 s, when your child forgot to add one of the laps. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The stopwatch · 407 s (letter C)
How to think: Turn the minutes into seconds (6 × 60) and add the 47. Calculation: 6 × 60 = 360 · 360 + 47 = 407 s.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many seconds are in 6 minutes? Is there something you forgot to add?”
Most common mistake: answering 647 s, when your child put the 6 and the 47 together as if they were one number. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The extra time · 30 s (letter E)
How to think: Comparing is finding the difference: 407 − 377. Calculation: 407 − 377 = 30 s.
If they get stuck, ask: “From 377 to 407, how many seconds are missing?”
Most common mistake: answering 784 s, when your child added the two times instead of comparing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: LACE. In the extra 30 seconds, Mia stopped to tie her sneaker LACE, and no one wrote it down. The stopwatch was right!
