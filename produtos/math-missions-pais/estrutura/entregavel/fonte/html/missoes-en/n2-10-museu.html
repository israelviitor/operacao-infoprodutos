The Great Midnight Museum Mystery

Time: 30 to 40 minutes · Practice: Level 2 Final Mission: 3 parts, times as many, fraction of a quantity, and division with a remainder · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

This is the level's Final Mission: all 4 stages are drawing stages, and each one uses one of the drawings your child has already mastered. In each stage, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use bottle caps and small containers to check stages 2 and 4 (groups of 45 and trips of 8) before each drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Here all 4 stages are drawing stages: set aside more time and, if needed, split the mission over 2 days.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your 4 drawings to me.” To move up a level, the code word has to work out and your child has to explain at least 3 of the 4 drawings on their own.
Before moving on: This is the Level 2 Final Mission. Your child moves on to Level 3 when the code word works out and they explain at least 3 of the 4 drawings on their own. If not yet, another day, redo just the drawings that were hard, with different numbers, and come back to this mission.

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Great Midnight Museum Mystery

Stage 1 — The night guard's rounds · 755 m (letter V)
How to think: There are 3 parts to the rounds. Add two, then the third. Calculation: 240 + 185 = 425 · 425 + 330 = 755 m.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Part-whole with 3 parts: the total is the sum of the 3 hallways.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many hallways are there? Did you add them all?”
Most common mistake: answering 425 m, when your child forgot to add the 3rd hallway. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The statue room · 180 (letter A)
How to think: “4 times” is 4 pieces equal to the 45 in the vase room. Calculation: 4 × 45 = 180.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Times as many: 4 pieces equal to the 45 in the vase room, 4 × 45.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many pieces of 45 fit in the statues bar?”
Most common mistake: answering 49, when your child added 4 instead of taking 4 times 45. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.

Stage 3 — The lights out · 108 (letter S)
How to think: Split 180 into 5 equal pieces and take 3. Calculation: 180 ÷ 5 = 36 · 3 × 36 = 108.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Each fifth is worth 180 ÷ 5 = 36. The 3 colored pieces are worth 108.
If they get stuck, ask: “How much is 1 of the 5 pieces worth? And 3?”
Most common mistake: answering 36, when your child found only 1/5 and forgot to take 3 pieces. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The cleaning cart · 5 (letter E)
How to think: Fill the cart 8 at a time until no more fit. What's left goes on the last trip. Calculation: 45 ÷ 8 = 5 full trips (40 pieces), remainder 5.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Equal pieces with some left over: 5 trips of 8 (40), and 45 − 40 = 5 are left.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many pieces go on 5 full trips? How many are left?”
Most common mistake: answering 6, when your child answered the number of trips, not the pieces on the last one. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: VASE. On the cart's last trip there were only 5 pieces, and one of them was the VASE. It wasn't stolen: it spent the night in the cleaning room and went back to its place in the morning.
