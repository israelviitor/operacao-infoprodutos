The Case of the Juice Factory

Time: 25 to 35 minutes · Practice: proportion through the unit: find 1 and then the total · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use bottles and a measuring cup (or identical cups): your child sees how many 500 mL cups fill 2 L. In stage 2, they set aside 2 small bottles per liter before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“first I found what 1 piece is worth”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 3 bottles = 1.5 L; how many in 6 L?).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Case of the Juice Factory

Stage 1 — The bottles · 2 L (letter L)
How to think: 4 bottles of 500 mL make 2,000 mL. Every 1,000 mL is 1 L. Calculation: 4 × 500 = 2,000 mL = 2 L.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many mL are in 1 L? And how many mL do the 4 bottles hold?”
Most common mistake: answering 20 L, when your child thought 1 L has 100 mL. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The machine's pace · 14 (letter I)
How to think: First find what 1 L is worth (2 bottles). Then, 7 L. Calculation: 4 ÷ 2 = 2 bottles per liter · 7 × 2 = 14.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Each piece is 1 bottle of 0.5 L. 14 pieces that size fit in 7 L.
If they get stuck, ask: “If 2 L fill 4 bottles, how many does 1 L fill?”
Most common mistake: answering 28, when your child multiplied 7 × 4, as if each liter filled 4 bottles. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — One hour of the machine · 120 (letter M)
How to think: Find how many it fills in 1 minute and then in 60. Calculation: 6 ÷ 3 = 2 bottles per minute · 60 × 2 = 120.
If they get stuck, ask: “In 1 minute, how many bottles? And in 60?”
Most common mistake: answering 360, when your child used 6 bottles per minute, not every 3 minutes. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The tank · 900 (letter E)
How to think: Each liter fills 2 half-liter bottles. 450 liters fill 450 × 2. Calculation: 1 L fills 2 bottles · 450 × 2 = 900.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many bottles does 1 L fill? And 450 L?”
Most common mistake: answering 225, when your child divided by 2 instead of multiplying (each liter fills 2 bottles). That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: LIME. The machine fills 2 bottles per minute, and the 450 L tank makes 900 bottles of LIME juice: a whole day of work.
