The Case of the Missing Stickers

Time: 20 to 25 minutes · Practice: subtraction: taking away and “how many are missing” · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use 48 little pieces of paper as stickers. In stage 2, your child sets aside 31 (the ones still stuck in) and sees how many are left before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words. If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 45 before, 28 today).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Case of the Missing Stickers

Stage 1 — Page 1 · 7 (letter P)
How to think: The whole page has 10 spots. 3 are taken. What's missing is 10 − 3. Calculation: 10 − 3 = 7.
If they get stuck, ask: “If 3 spots are filled, how many are empty?” Counting on your fingers from 3 to 10 also works.
Most common mistake: answering 13, when your child added instead of seeing how many are missing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The stickers that came out · 17 (letter A)
How to think: The 48 from before are the whole. The 31 that stayed are one part. The other part is what came out: 48 − 31. Calculation: 48 − 31 = 17.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Knowing the whole (48) and one part (31), the other part is what's missing: 48 − 31.
If they get stuck, ask: “There were 48 and now there are 31. Did more or fewer than 20 come out?”
Most common mistake: answering 79, when your child added instead of subtracting. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The stickers that were found · 5 (letter W)
How to think: Of the 17 that came out, 12 have already turned up. The missing ones are what's left: 17 − 12. Calculation: 17 − 12 = 5.
If they get stuck, ask: “If 12 have already come back, how many of the 17 are still missing?”
Most common mistake: answering 29, when your child put the found ones together with the ones that came out. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The marks on the couch · 12 (letter S)
How to think: Each print has 4 little toes. There are 3 prints: 4 + 4 + 4. Calculation: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12.
If they get stuck, ask: “Draw the 3 paws with 4 little toes each. How many little toes did you draw?”
Most common mistake: answering 3, when your child counted the prints, not the toes. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: PAWS. It was the cat, PAWS and all! The 5 missing stickers were under the cushion where it sleeps, along with the little paw prints.
