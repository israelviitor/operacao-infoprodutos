The Mystery of the Game Shop

Time: 25 to 35 minutes · Practice: simple percentages: 10%, 25%, and 50% in game points · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use a strip of paper: fold it into 10 (10%), into 4 (25%), and into 2 (50%). In stage 2, your child folds the strip into 4 and crosses out 1 part before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“25% is 1 of 4 pieces”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., a 25% discount on 360 points).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Mystery of the Game Shop

Stage 1 — The sword's discount · 30 (letter H)
How to think: 10% is 1 of 10 equal pieces: 300 ÷ 10. Calculation: 10% = 1/10 · 300 ÷ 10 = 30.
If they get stuck, ask: “If 300 were divided into 10 pieces, what would one be worth?”
Most common mistake: answering 290, when your child took off 10 points instead of 10%. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The shield · 360 (letter E)
How to think: 25% is 1 of 4 pieces. Find the piece (the discount) and take it off the price. Calculation: 25% = 1/4 · 480 ÷ 4 = 120 · 480 − 120 = 360.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The shaded piece is the discount (480 ÷ 4 = 120). The new price is the 3 white pieces: 360.
If they get stuck, ask: “Is the question the discount or the price after the discount?”
Most common mistake: answering 120, when your child answered the discount, not the new price. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The helm · 125 (letter L)
How to think: 50% is half: 250 ÷ 2. Calculation: 50% = half · 250 ÷ 2 = 125.
If they get stuck, ask: “What is half of 250?”
Most common mistake: answering 200, when your child took off 50 points instead of 50%. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The cart · 250 (letter M)
How to think: The cart is the whole. The sword and the shield are known parts. The helm is what's missing. Calculation: 270 + 360 = 630 · 880 − 630 = 250.
If they get stuck, ask: “How much do the sword and the shield cost together? How much is missing to reach 880?”
Most common mistake: answering 630, when your child answered the sum of the other two items. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: HELM. The cart charged 250 points for the HELM, without the 50% discount. With the right price (125), the cart total adds up.
