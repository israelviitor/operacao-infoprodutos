The Campout Treasure Hunt

Time: 25 to 30 minutes · Practice: multiplication, making change with game coins, fractions, and division · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Set aside 60 bottle caps or dried beans in 6 piles of 10: these are the gold coins. In stage 2, your child sets aside what they paid for the flashlight and the rope and counts what's left before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words. If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 50 coins, a flashlight for 28, a rope for 15).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Campout Treasure Hunt

Stage 1 — The starting ropes · 12 m (letter L)
How to think: Each rope is 3 meters long. There are 4 ropes: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3. Calculation: 4 ropes × 3 meters = 12 meters.
If they get stuck, ask: “How long is one rope? And four ropes?” You can draw the 4 ropes and write 3 on each one.
Most common mistake: answering 7 m, when your child added 4 + 3 instead of putting together 4 ropes of 3 meters. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The camp store · 8 (letter A)
How to think: First, how much the two things cost together (35 + 17). Then, how much is left of the 60 coins. Calculation: 35 + 17 = 52 · 60 − 52 = 8.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The drawing shows the 60 coins split into 3 pieces. If you know 2 pieces, the third is what's left: 60 − 35 − 17 (or 60 − 52).
If they get stuck, ask: “How much did you spend in all? And how much did you have at the start?”
Most common mistake: answering 25, when your child forgot to pay for the rope. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The trail map · 300 m (letter K)
How to think: Split the trail into 4 equal parts (100 meters each) and walk 3 of those parts. Calculation: 400 ÷ 4 = 100 meters in each part · 3 parts = 300 meters.
If they get stuck, ask: “If the trail were cut into 4 equal pieces, how long would each piece be? And 3 pieces?” Drawing a line helps.
Most common mistake: answering 100 m, when your child found only 1/4 of the trail and forgot to multiply by 3. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The chest · 21 (letter E)
How to think: Share 84 into 4 equal parts. You can break it up: 80 ÷ 4 = 20 and 4 ÷ 4 = 1. Calculation: 84 ÷ 4 = 21 (80 ÷ 4 = 20 and 4 ÷ 4 = 1).
If they get stuck, ask: “If there were 8 coins for 4 people, how many would each one get? And 80?” Beans or bottle caps help with sharing.
Most common mistake: answering 80, when your child subtracted 4 instead of sharing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: LAKE. The chest was behind the big rock, at the edge of the LAKE, 300 meters from the start. And the 84 gold coins were split equally among the explorers!
