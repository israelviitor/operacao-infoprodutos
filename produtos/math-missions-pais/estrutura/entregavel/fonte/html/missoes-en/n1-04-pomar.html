The Riddle of the Neighbor's Orchard

Time: 20 to 25 minutes · Practice: multiplication: equal groups (×2, ×5, ×10) · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use 6 small containers (or cups) as baskets and 5 dried beans in each one. In stage 2, your child counts the beans by 5s before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“each piece is a basket”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 4 baskets with 10).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Riddle of the Neighbor's Orchard

Stage 1 — The oranges · 20 (letter P)
How to think: Each basket has 10. There are 2 baskets: 10 + 10. Calculation: 2 × 10 = 20 (or 10 + 10).
If they get stuck, ask: “How many oranges are in one basket? What about in two?”
Most common mistake: answering 12, when your child added 2 + 10 instead of putting together 2 baskets of 10. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The apples · 30 (letter E)
How to think: There are 6 equal groups of 5. Count by 5s six times. Calculation: 6 × 5 = 30 (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30).
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Equal pieces with the ? on the total: it's multiplication (6 × 5), or adding 5 six times.
If they get stuck, ask: “Count by 5s, once for each basket: 5, 10, 15…”
Most common mistake: answering 11, when your child added 6 + 5 instead of putting together 6 baskets of 5. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — Everything in the baskets · 50 (letter A)
How to think: The fruits are 20 oranges and 30 apples: 20 + 30. Calculation: 20 + 30 = 50.
If they get stuck, ask: “Is the question about baskets or about fruits?”
Most common mistake: answering 8, when your child counted the baskets, not the fruits. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The missing fruits · 10 (letter R)
How to think: The notebook says 60. The baskets have 50. What's missing is 60 − 50. Calculation: 60 − 50 = 10.
If they get stuck, ask: “From 50 to 60, how many are missing?”
Most common mistake: answering 110, when your child added instead of seeing how many are missing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: PEAR. 10 PEARS went missing: their basket fell behind the wall in the wind. They were there, all in one piece, and the neighbor's count worked out to 60.
