The Mystery of the Heavy Backpack

Time: 25 to 30 minutes · Practice: mass in kilograms and grams; 2-step problem · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use a kitchen scale, if you have one: weigh a 500 g bag of rice or beans. In stage 2, your child sets aside 3 small piles of bottle caps (snack, water, and jacket) before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“the bar is the backpack; I split it into 3 parts”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 250 g, 400 g, and 150 g).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Mystery of the Heavy Backpack

Stage 1 — Half a kilo · 500 g (letter Y)
How to think: Half a kilo is half of 1,000 g: 1,000 ÷ 2. Calculation: 1,000 ÷ 2 = 500 g.
If they get stuck, ask: “If 1 kg is 1,000 g, what is half of that?”
Most common mistake: answering 1,000 g, when your child forgot to split the kilo in half. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — What Theo says he brought · 950 g (letter O)
How to think: There are 3 parts of the backpack. Add two, then the third. Calculation: 350 + 420 = 770 · 770 + 180 = 950 g.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Part-whole with 3 parts and the ? on the total: add the parts, two at a time.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many things are there? Did you add them all?”
Most common mistake: answering 770 g, when your child forgot to add the jacket. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — What the scale showed · 50 g (letter Y)
How to think: The scale shows everything. The 3 things weigh 950. The rest is 1,000 − 950. Calculation: 1,000 − 950 = 50 g.
If they get stuck, ask: “From 950 to 1,000, how many grams are missing?”
Most common mistake: answering 1,950 g, when your child added instead of finding the difference. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — To pass the scale · 200 g (letter O)
How to think: The backpack has 1,000 and can have 800. The difference has to come out: 1,000 − 800. Calculation: 1,000 − 800 = 200 g.
If they get stuck, ask: “How much over the limit was the backpack?”
Most common mistake: answering 1,800 g, when your child added the limit to the weight of the backpack. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: YOYO. The extra 50 g was a wooden YOYO forgotten in the front pocket! Theo took out the yo-yo and left his jacket with his mom: the backpack came in under 800 g.
