The Case of the Marble Jar

Time: 25 to 35 minutes · Practice: fraction of what was left (1/3 and then 1/4 of the rest) · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use a strip of paper divided into 6 equal parts: your child crosses out 2 parts (1/3) and then 1 part of the 4 that are left (1/4 of the rest), before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“the 1/4 is of what was left, not of the whole jar”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 180 marbles).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Case of the Marble Jar

Stage 1 — The 1st day · 80 (letter L)
How to think: 1/3 is 1 of the 3 equal pieces: 240 ÷ 3. Calculation: 240 ÷ 3 = 80.
If they get stuck, ask: “Divide 240 into 3 equal parts. How many are in each one?”
Most common mistake: answering 237, when your child subtracted 3 instead of finding 1/3. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The 2nd day · 120 (letter O)
How to think: First, what was left after the 1st day (160). The 1/4 is of that rest. Calculation: 240 − 80 = 160 · 160 ÷ 4 = 40 · 160 − 40 = 120.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The 1st day takes 1/3 (80). The rest (160) is divided into 4 pieces of 40; 1 is lent on the 2nd day. 3 pieces are left: 120.
If they get stuck, ask: “Is the 1/4 of the 240 or of the ones that were left?”
Most common mistake: answering 100, when your child found 1/4 of the whole jar, not of what was left. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The ones that came back · 36 (letter A)
How to think: Divide 120 into 10 parts and take 3. Calculation: 120 ÷ 10 = 12 · 3 × 12 = 36.
If they get stuck, ask: “What is 1/10 of 120? And 3/10?”
Most common mistake: answering 12, when your child found only 1/10 and forgot to take 3 pieces. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The jar now · 156 (letter N)
How to think: The ones that came back join the ones that were in the jar. Calculation: 120 + 36 = 156.
If they get stuck, ask: “Is going back into the jar putting together or taking away?”
Most common mistake: answering 84, when your child subtracted instead of adding the ones that came back. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: LOAN. The jar ended up with 156 marbles. The other 84 are still on LOAN, and now the family writes down every loan.
