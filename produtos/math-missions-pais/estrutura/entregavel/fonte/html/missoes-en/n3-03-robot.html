The Riddle of the Robot Vacuum

Time: 25 to 35 minutes · Practice: fractions, decimals, patterns in a table, and proportion · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 4 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
A strip of paper helps: folded into 4, it shows 3/4 of the battery (stage 1). For stage 4, cut out small paper squares: each one is 1 minute of cleaning.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 4 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 4 in their own words. If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 15 m² in 3 minutes, a 45 m² living room).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Riddle of the Robot Vacuum

Stage 1 — The battery · 75 (letter C)
How to think: Split 100 into 4 parts (25 each) and take 3 of them. Calculation: 100 ÷ 4 = 25 · 3 × 25 = 75.
If they get stuck, ask: “What is one quarter of 100? And three quarters?” Drawing a bar split into 4 parts helps.
Most common mistake: answering 25, when your child calculated only 1/4 (or what's missing to fill it up). That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The robot's path · 4.25 m (letter O)
How to think: Write 2.4 as 2.40 and add with the decimal points lined up: 2.40 + 1.85. Calculation: 2.40 + 1.85 = 4.25.
If they get stuck, ask: “Is 2.4 the same as 2.40? Now add centimeters to centimeters.”
Most common mistake: answering 3.89 m, when your child added without lining up the decimal points (did 4 + 85 = 89). That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The energy log · 5 min (letter R)
How to think: Find out how much the energy drops per minute (15 points) and continue the table until it reaches zero. Calculation: uses 15 points per minute · 75 ÷ 15 = 5 minutes (or continue the table: minute 4 = 15, minute 5 = 0).
If they get stuck, ask: “How much does the energy drop from one minute to the next? Continue the table: minute 4, minute 5…”
Most common mistake: answering 4 min, when your child counted only 1 more minute after minute 3. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The whole room · 10 min (letter D)
How to think: Find out how much it cleans in 1 minute (12 ÷ 3 = 4 m²) and how many minutes it takes to do 40 m². Calculation: 12 ÷ 3 = 4 m² per minute · 40 ÷ 4 = 10 minutes.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Each piece is 1 minute of cleaning (4 m²). To cover 40 m², you need 10 pieces, which means 10 minutes.
If they get stuck, ask: “Draw a bar with 3 equal pieces that together are worth 12 m². How much is 1 piece worth? Now draw pieces that size until you reach 40 m². How many pieces?”
Most common mistake: answering 3 min, when your child repeated the time from the example, without seeing that the room is bigger. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: CORD. The charger CORD was partly loose: the battery never got above 3/4 and lasted only 5 minutes — half of the 10 minutes the living room needs. That's why the robot cleaned half and stopped. Cord back in place, robot fixed!
