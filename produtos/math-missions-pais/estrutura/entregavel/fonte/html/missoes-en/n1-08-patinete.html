The Scooter Track in the Square

Time: 20 to 25 minutes · Practice: measuring in meters: adding sections and how much is missing · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 3 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use a tape measure or steps: in the hallway, mark 2 meters on the floor with tape. In stage 3, your child sets aside 9 little piles of 10 dried beans (the 90 m track), takes away 6 piles and 3 more beans (the 63 m), and counts what's left before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 3 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 3 in their own words. If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., an 80 m track, two sections that add up to 52 m).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Scooter Track in the Square

Stage 1 — The pushes · 20 m (letter L)
How to think: Each push is worth 2 meters. There are 10 pushes: 2 + 2 + … ten times. Calculation: 10 × 2 = 20 meters.
If they get stuck, ask: “How far does one push go? What about two? And ten?”
Most common mistake: answering 12 m, when your child added 10 + 2 instead of putting together 10 pushes of 2 meters. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The first two sections · 63 m (letter I)
How to think: The sections come one after the other: the length is the sum. Calculation: 35 + 28 = 63 meters.
If they get stuck, ask: “If you ride the 1st section and then the 2nd, how far did you go in all?”
Most common mistake: answering 7 m, when your child took away instead of putting the sections together. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The faded section · 27 m (letter N)
How to think: The whole track is the whole (90). The two sections are one part (63). The faded section is the missing part. Calculation: 90 − 63 = 27 meters.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
With the whole and one part known, the missing part is 90 − 63.
If they get stuck, ask: “From 63 to 90, how many meters are missing?”
Most common mistake: answering 153 m, when your child added instead of seeing what's missing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — Almost at the finish · 15 m (letter E)
How to think: The track is 90. He has already gone 75. What's left is 90 − 75. Calculation: 90 − 75 = 15 meters.
If they get stuck, ask: “From 75 to 90, how many meters?”
Most common mistake: answering 165 m, when your child added what he had gone to the whole track. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: LINE. The section with the faded sign is 27 m long: it's the last one, the one that reaches the finish LINE. The new sign has already been painted with the right length.
