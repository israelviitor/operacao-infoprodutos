The Mystery of the Backyard Scoreboard

Time: 20 to 25 minutes · Practice: comparison: how many more, how many fewer · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 3 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Make two rows of bottle caps, one under the other: 23 for Blue and 15 for Green. In stage 3, your child sees how many of Blue's bottle caps are left without a pair before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 3 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 3 in their own words (“this bar is Blue's, this one is Green's, and the dashed piece is the difference”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 27 and 18).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Mystery of the Backyard Scoreboard

Stage 1 — Blue's goals · 7 (letter B)
How to think: The goals from the two halves go together: 3 + 4. Calculation: 3 + 4 = 7.
If they get stuck, ask: “The game has two halves. Do the goals from both count?”
Most common mistake: answering 1, when your child took away instead of putting together. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — Green's goals · 5 (letter L)
How to think: “Fewer” means Green scored less than Blue's 7: 7 − 2. Calculation: 7 − 2 = 5.
If they get stuck, ask: “Did Green score more or fewer goals than Blue?”
Most common mistake: answering 9, when your child read “fewer” and added. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The shots on target · 8 (letter U)
How to think: Comparing is seeing what the bigger bar has that the smaller one doesn't: 23 − 15. Calculation: 23 − 15 = 8.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The two bars lined up show that the dashed piece is what Green needs to reach Blue. That's why the math is 23 − 15.
If they get stuck, ask: “If Green made a few more shots, it would be even with Blue. How many?”
Most common mistake: answering 38, when your child added the two teams instead of comparing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — Blue's points · 30 (letter E)
How to think: The points are goals and shots on target together: 7 + 23. Calculation: 7 + 23 = 30.
If they get stuck, ask: “Tournament points = goals + shots on target. How much is that?”
Most common mistake: answering 16, when your child took away instead of putting together. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: BLUE. Team BLUE won the tournament: they scored more goals and made more shots on target. The chalk scoreboard was redone on the wall, now with tape over it!
