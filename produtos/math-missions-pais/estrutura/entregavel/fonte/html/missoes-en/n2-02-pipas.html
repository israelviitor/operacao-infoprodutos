The Case of the Kite Contest

Time: 25 to 30 minutes · Practice: comparison with a total ("more than" and "both together") · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use two pieces of string: one 15 hand spans long (the blue spool) and another one 6 hand spans longer (the red one). In stage 2, your child stretches the two out side by side before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“the red bar is the blue one plus a piece”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., blue 120 m; red 45 m more).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Case of the Kite Contest

Stage 1 — The height of the kites · 40 m (letter W)
How to think: “More” is the difference between the two: 120 − 80. Calculation: 120 − 80 = 40 m.
If they get stuck, ask: “How much more does the blue one need to go up to match the red one?”
Most common mistake: answering 200 m, when your child added the two heights instead of comparing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The spools · 360 m (letter A)
How to think: First, the red one (150 + 60). Then, the two together. Calculation: 150 + 60 = 210 · 150 + 210 = 360 m.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The drawing shows 2 equal pieces of 150 plus 60: 150 + 150 + 60. The ? goes on the side, adding up the two bars.
If they get stuck, ask: “How much does the red one have? And the two together?”
Most common mistake: answering 210 m, when your child found only the red spool and forgot to put it together with the blue one. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The string that got away · 120 m (letter L)
How to think: The spool had 210. 90 stayed. What went away is 210 − 90. Calculation: 210 − 90 = 120 m.
If they get stuck, ask: “If 90 of the 210 stayed, how much came off the spool?”
Most common mistake: answering 300 m, when your child added what was left to the full spool. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The kite's flight · 195 m (letter L)
How to think: The two parts of the flight go together: 120 + 75. Calculation: 120 + 75 = 195 m.
If they get stuck, ask: “It went 120 and then another 75. How much is that in all?”
Most common mistake: answering 45 m, when your child subtracted instead of putting together the two parts of the flight. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: WALL. The kite flew 195 m and fell on the other side of the park WALL. It was found caught in the climbing vine, all in one piece.
