The Case of the Rectangular Garden

Time: 25 to 35 minutes · Practice: perimeter (m) and area (m²) · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 3 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
On a sheet of graph paper, your child draws the garden 12 squares long and 8 squares wide and counts the squares inside (area) and the sides outside (perimeter).
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 3 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 3 in their own words. If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., a 10 m by 6 m garden, 27.5 m of wire fencing).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Case of the Rectangular Garden

Stage 1 — The garden's area · 96 m² (letter W)
How to think: The area counts the 1 m² squares inside: 12 rows of 8. Calculation: 12 × 8 = 96 m².
If they get stuck, ask: “On the graph paper, how many squares fit inside the garden?”
Most common mistake: answering 20 m², when your child added the sides instead of multiplying. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The vegetable patch · 48 m² (letter I)
How to think: Half is dividing into 2 equal parts: 96 ÷ 2. Calculation: 96 ÷ 2 = 48 m².
If they get stuck, ask: “If the garden were divided into 2 equal parts, how much would each one have?”
Most common mistake: answering 94 m², when your child subtracted 2 instead of dividing in half. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The missing fencing · 5.5 m (letter R)
How to think: First the whole way around (the 4 sides). It is the whole; the fencing is one part; what's missing is the other. Calculation: 12 + 8 + 12 + 8 = 40 · 40.0 − 34.5 = 5.5 m.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The perimeter (12 + 8 + 12 + 8 = 40 m) is the whole; the fencing that arrived is one part. What's missing is 40 − 34.5.
If they get stuck, ask: “How long is the way around the garden? From 34.5 up to that, how many meters are missing?”
Most common mistake: answering 74.5 m, when your child added the fencing to the way around instead of seeing what's missing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The rolls · 3 (letter E)
How to think: Add 2.5 m rolls until you go past 5.5 m. Calculation: 2 rolls = 5 m (not enough) · 3 rolls = 7.5 m: 3 rolls.
If they get stuck, ask: “With 2 rolls, how many meters do you have? Is it enough?”
Most common mistake: answering 2, when your child bought 2 rolls, which make only 5 m and don't close the fence. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: WIRE. 5.5 m of WIRE fencing was missing: the store sent 34.5 m, but the garden is 40 m all the way around. With 3 more rolls, the fence was closed.
