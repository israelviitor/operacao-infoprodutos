The Secret of the Two Contest Pumpkins

Time: 25 to 35 minutes · Practice: sum and difference: finding the two parts · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use two pieces of string: one longer than the other. In stage 2, your child cuts the “extra” piece off the longer one and sees that two equal pieces are left, before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“I took away the extra piece and two equal bars were left”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 7 kg together, one weighs 0.8 kg more).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Secret of the Two Contest Pumpkins

Stage 1 — Kilograms into grams · 1,400 g (letter M)
How to think: 1 kg has 1,000 g. 1.4 kg is 1,000 g plus 4 tenths of 1,000 (400 g). Calculation: 1.4 × 1,000 = 1,400 g.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many grams are in 1 kg? And in 0.4 kg?”
Most common mistake: answering 140 g, when your child multiplied by 100 instead of 1,000. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The green pumpkin · 3.8 kg (letter A)
How to think: Take away the “extra” piece (1.4). What's left is two equal bars: divide by 2. Calculation: 9 − 1.4 = 7.6 · 7.6 ÷ 2 = 3.8 kg.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Taking away the 1.4 kg piece, 2 equal bars are left that together weigh 7.6 kg. Each one is worth 3.8 kg.
If they get stuck, ask: “If the orange one didn't have the extra 1.4, the two would be equal. How much would they weigh together?”
Most common mistake: answering 7.6 kg, when your child subtracted the 1.4 and forgot to divide by 2. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The orange pumpkin · 5.2 kg (letter S)
How to think: The orange one is the green one plus the extra piece. Calculation: 3.8 + 1.4 = 5.2 kg.
If they get stuck, ask: “Is the orange one heavier or lighter than the green one?”
Most common mistake: answering 2.4 kg, when your child subtracted the extra piece instead of adding it. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The record · 0.15 kg (letter S)
How to think: Write 5.2 as 5.20 and subtract with the decimal points lined up. Calculation: 5.20 − 5.05 = 0.15 kg.
If they get stuck, ask: “Is 5.2 the same as 5.20? How much is missing from 5.05 up to 5.20?”
Most common mistake: answering 10.25 kg, when your child added instead of comparing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: MASS. The orange pumpkin won the trophy: 5.2 kg, the contest's new record. The right MASS came out of the drawing of the two bars.
