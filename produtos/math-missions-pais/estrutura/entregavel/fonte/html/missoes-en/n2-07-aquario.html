The Case of the Leaky Fish Tank

Time: 25 to 30 minutes · Practice: capacity in liters and milliliters; converting and finding the difference · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 3 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use a measuring cup or bottles: a 2 L bottle and a 250 mL cup. In stage 3, your child pours water out of the bottle and sees what's left before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 3 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 3 in their own words. If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., a 1 L pitcher, 350 mL left over).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Case of the Leaky Fish Tank

Stage 1 — Half a liter · 500 mL (letter D)
How to think: Half a liter is half of 1,000 mL. Calculation: 1,000 ÷ 2 = 500 mL.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many mL are in 1 L? And half?”
Most common mistake: answering 50 mL, when your child thought 1 L is 100 mL. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The level that goes down · 1,000 mL (letter R)
How to think: There are 4 equal days of 250 mL: 250 + 250 + 250 + 250. Calculation: 4 × 250 = 1,000 mL.
If they get stuck, ask: “How much does it go down in 1 day? And in 2? And in 4?”
Most common mistake: answering 254 mL, when your child added 4 instead of putting together 4 days of 250 mL. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The pitcher · 1,250 mL (letter O)
How to think: Turn 2 L into 2,000 mL. The pitcher is the whole; what was left is one part; what went into the fish tank is the other. Calculation: 2 L = 2,000 mL · 2,000 − 750 = 1,250 mL.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Part-whole with mL: the whole is 2,000 mL, one part is 750 mL, and the missing part is 2,000 − 750.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many mL did the pitcher have? And how many stayed in it?”
Most common mistake: answering 2,750 mL, when your child added what was left to the full pitcher. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The lid · 200 mL (letter P)
How to think: Before, 250; now, 50. The difference is 250 − 50. Calculation: 250 − 50 = 200 mL.
If they get stuck, ask: “How much less does the fish tank lose per day now?”
Most common mistake: answering 300 mL, when your child added the two measurements instead of comparing. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: DROP. It wasn't a leak: it was the water evaporating. With a lid, every DROP that evaporates goes back into the tank, and the level hardly goes down anymore.
