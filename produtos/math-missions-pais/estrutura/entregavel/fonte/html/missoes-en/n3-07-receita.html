The Riddle of the Giant Recipe

Time: 25 to 35 minutes · Practice: unequal parts (ratio 3 : 2) with a total in grams · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use identical small cups: 3 cups of rice (flour) and 2 of coarse salt (sugar). In stage 2, your child sees that the mix has 5 equal cups before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words (“there are 5 equal pieces: 3 of flour and 2 of sugar”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 1,000 g in the ratio 3 : 2).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Riddle of the Giant Recipe

Stage 1 — The recipe's eggs · 9 (letter E)
How to think: Tripling is taking it 3 times: 3 + 3 + 3. Calculation: 3 × 3 = 9.
If they get stuck, ask: “If you make the recipe 3 times, how many eggs do you use?”
Most common mistake: answering 6, when your child added 3 instead of tripling. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The flour · 900 g (letter G)
How to think: The mix has 5 equal parts (3 + 2). Find what 1 part is worth and take 3. Calculation: 3 + 2 = 5 parts · 1,500 ÷ 5 = 300 · 3 × 300 = 900 g.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
There are 5 equal pieces that together weigh 1,500 g. Each piece is worth 300 g; the flour is 3 pieces.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many parts does the mix have in all? What is each one worth?”
Most common mistake: answering 500 g, when your child divided by 3, without counting the 5 parts of the mix. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The sugar · 600 g (letter G)
How to think: The sugar is 2 of the 5 pieces of 300 g. Calculation: 2 × 300 = 600 g (or 1,500 − 900).
If they get stuck, ask: “How many 300 g pieces are sugar?”
Most common mistake: answering 750 g, when your child split the mix in half, as if the parts were equal. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The eggs in the batter · 3 (letter S)
How to think: How many times does 55 fit into 165? Add 55 until you reach 165. Calculation: 165 ÷ 55 = 3 (55 + 55 + 55 = 165).
If they get stuck, ask: “How much do 2 eggs weigh? And 3?”
Most common mistake: answering 110, when your child subtracted one egg instead of seeing how many 55 g eggs fit into 165 g. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: EGGS. The mistake was in the EGGS: the flour and the sugar were tripled, but the eggs weren't. The right recipe takes 9 eggs, and the cake times 3 turned out right on the second try.
