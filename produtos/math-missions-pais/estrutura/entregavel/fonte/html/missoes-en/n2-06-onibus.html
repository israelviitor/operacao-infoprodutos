The Mystery of the Field Trip Bus

Time: 25 to 30 minutes · Practice: division with a remainder: how many groups fit and how many are left over · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 3 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use bottle caps as people and boxes (or plates) as buses. In stage 3, your child fills the buses 36 at a time (they can use piles of 10 and of 6) and sees what's left over before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 3 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 3 in their own words (“3 full pieces and a little piece left over”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 100 people, buses with 30 seats).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Mystery of the Field Trip Bus

Stage 1 — The seats on the bus · 36 (letter V)
How to think: There are 9 equal rows of 4 seats: 9 × 4. Calculation: 9 × 4 = 36.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many seats are in one row? And in nine?”
Most common mistake: answering 13, when your child added 9 + 4 instead of putting together 9 rows of 4. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The trip there · 108 (letter A)
How to think: There are 3 equal buses of 36: 36 + 36 + 36. Calculation: 3 × 36 = 108.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many people fit on one bus? And on three?”
Most common mistake: answering 39, when your child added 3 instead of putting together 3 buses of 36. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The trip back · 19 (letter N)
How to think: Fill the buses 36 at a time until no more fit. What didn't fit is the remainder. Calculation: 127 ÷ 36 = 3 full buses (108), remainder 19.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
Equal pieces with some left over: 3 pieces of 36 fit (108), and the little piece left over is 127 − 108 = 19.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many people fit on the 3 buses? And how many are left outside?”
Most common mistake: answering 4, when your child answered how many buses would be needed, not how many people are left over. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The vans · 3 (letter S)
How to think: Two vans carry 16. 3 people are left, and they need to go too. Calculation: 19 ÷ 8 = 2, remainder 3 · the 3 left over need 1 more van: 3 vans.
If they get stuck, ask: “With 2 vans, did everyone go? Who was left?”
Most common mistake: answering 2, when your child forgot the 3 people in the remainder, who also need a seat. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: VANS. On the way back there were 127 people: 3 full buses and 19 left over. The club called 3 VANS, and no one was left behind.
