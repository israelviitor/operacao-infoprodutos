The Mystery of the Torn Graph

Time: 25 to 30 minutes · Practice: table and bar graph; comparing; pattern in a table · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 3 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Build the columns of the graph with stacked bottle caps (1 cap = 1 vote, or 1 cap = 5 votes). In stage 3, your child puts Trovão's and Faísca's columns side by side before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 3 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 3 in their own words. If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 42 and 35 votes).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Mystery of the Torn Graph

Stage 1 — The piece that was left · 61 (letter V)
How to think: The votes for the two names go together: 34 + 27. Calculation: 34 + 27 = 61.
If they get stuck, ask: “Does the question want the two together or the difference?”
Most common mistake: answering 7, when your child subtracted instead of putting together. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The torn column · 39 (letter O)
How to think: The 100 votes are the whole. 61 are already known. Trovão is the rest: 100 − 61. Calculation: 100 − 61 = 39.
If they get stuck, ask: “From 61 to 100, how many votes are missing?”
Most common mistake: answering 161, when your child added instead of finding what's missing to make 100. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The winner · 5 (letter T)
How to think: First find 1st and 2nd place. Then compare the two columns. Calculation: 1st Trovão (39), 2nd Faísca (34) · 39 − 34 = 5.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The columns of the graph become bars. The dashed piece is the difference: 39 − 34.
If they get stuck, ask: “How many votes did Faísca need to tie?”
Most common mistake: answering 12, when your child compared with last place, not with 2nd. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The pattern in the ballot box · 49 (letter E)
How to think: Find out how much it goes up from one hour to the next (10) and continue the table. Calculation: goes up 10 each hour: 11 a.m. → 29, 12 p.m. → 39, 1 p.m. → 49.
If they get stuck, ask: “From 9 to 19, how much did it go up? And from 19 to 29? Keep going: 12 p.m., 1 p.m.”
Most common mistake: answering 40, when your child added 1 instead of 10 each hour. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: VOTE. Trovão won, with 39 VOTES, 5 more than Faísca. The graph was redone and hung on the club's bulletin board.
