The Mystery of the Library Clock

Time: 20 to 25 minutes · Practice: time: duration in minutes, start and end · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 2 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Use an analog clock (or draw one). In stage 2, your child turns the long hand 35 minutes and then 25 more, and sees how many times it went around before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 2 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 2 in their own words. If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 40 minutes of story and 20 of drawing).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Mystery of the Library Clock

Stage 1 — The wall clock · 4:00 (letter B)
How to think: The short hand shows the hour. The long hand on the 12 means “o'clock.” Calculation: short hand on the 4 = 4 hours; long hand on the 12 = o'clock: 4:00.
If they get stuck, ask: “Which hand shows the hour: the short one or the long one?”
Most common mistake: answering 12:20, when your child mixed up the hands (read the long one as the hour and the short one as the minutes). That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The two parts of the session · 60 min (letter E)
How to think: The two parts, one after the other, make up the whole session: 35 + 25. Calculation: 35 + 25 = 60 minutes.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The bar is a timeline: the two parts together make up the session. With the ? on the whole, the math is 35 + 25.
If they get stuck, ask: “The session is the story and the drawing together. How much time is that?”
Most common mistake: answering 10 min, when your child took away instead of putting the two parts together. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The start of the session · 3:00 (letter L)
How to think: If it ended at 4:00 and lasted 1 hour, it started 1 hour earlier. Calculation: 4:00 minus 1 hour = 3:00.
If they get stuck, ask: “Did the session start before or after it ended?” Move the short hand back 1 number.
Most common mistake: answering 5:00, when your child went forward 1 hour instead of going back. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — The library bell · 3 (letter L)
How to think: List the times: 3:00, 3:30, 4:00. Count the rings. Calculation: 3:00, 3:30, and 4:00 = 3 times.
If they get stuck, ask: “Write down all the times the bell rings between 3:00 and 4:00. How many are there?”
Most common mistake: answering 2, when your child forgot to count one of the ends (the 3:00 ring). That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: BELL. The session started at 3:00 p.m., and the library BELL confirmed it: it rang 3 times during the reading. The report is done!
