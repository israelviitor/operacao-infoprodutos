The Vanishing Cookie Mystery

Time: 20 to 25 minutes · Practice: multiplication (equal rows), subtraction, and telling time · Materials: pencil, eraser, and this printed sheet (optional: analog clock, dried beans, or bottle caps).

The method in this mission: draw before you solve

Stage 3 is the “draw the problem” stage. In it, your child goes through the 3 steps of the Singapore method (details in the Parent Guide):

1. Concrete
Set aside 20 dried beans, bottle caps, or paper balls: these are the cookies. In stage 3, your child puts 13 in the “tin” and sees what's left before drawing.
2. Drawing
Your child draws the bar and puts a ? on what they want to find out. Only then do they do the math.
3. Calculation
With the drawing done, they write the equation and check the answer by looking at the drawing.

How to run the mission

  1. Read the opening out loud, with an air of mystery. Stage 1 is very short, on purpose: it gets your child into the game quickly.
  2. Don't say which operation to use. The problem doesn't say whether to add, subtract, or multiply. Figuring that out is the exercise, and the drawing is what shows the answer.
  3. If your child gets stuck, ask for the drawing before giving any help: “Show me that in a bar.” Then use the support question from the answer key (last page). It helps without giving away the answer.
  4. Each answer becomes a letter. If the code word doesn't make sense, one of the calculations is wrong: go back to the drawing and look for it together.
  5. Wrap up by asking: “Explain your drawing from stage 3 to me.”
Before moving on: You can move on to the next mission when your child explains the drawing in stage 3 in their own words (“this part is…”). If not yet, redo just that stage another day with different numbers (e.g., 25 baked, 16 in the tin).

With siblings

Print the mission pages once and solve them together, switching roles at each stage: reader (reads out loud), illustrator (draws the bar), and number cruncher (does the math and checks it against the drawing). With 2 children, the reader also draws. If they're at different levels, each one does the mission for their level and, at the end, they tell each other how they drew it.

Answer key: The Vanishing Cookie Mystery

Stage 1 — The lock on the tin · 8 (letter N)
How to think: Each cat has 4 paws. There are 2 cats: 4 + 4. Calculation: 2 cats × 4 paws = 8 (or 4 + 4).
If they get stuck, ask: “How many paws does one cat have? What if there are two cats?” If it helps, draw the cats together.
Most common mistake: answering 2, when your child counted the cats, not the paws. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 2 — The baking sheet · 20 (letter A)
How to think: There are 4 equal rows. Each row has 5: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5. Calculation: 4 rows × 5 = 20 (or 5 + 5 + 5 + 5).
If they get stuck, ask: “Draw the baking sheet: 4 rows with 5 dots. How many dots did you draw?”
Most common mistake: answering 9, when your child added 4 + 5 instead of putting together 4 rows of 5. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 3 — The lighter tin · 7 (letter N)
How to think: There were 20 and now there are 13. The difference is what went missing: 20 − 13. Calculation: 20 − 13 = 7.
The expected drawing (any drawing with the same parts counts):
The drawing shows that the missing part is what it takes to get from 13 to 20. That's why it's subtraction (or a “how many more to get there”).
If they get stuck, ask: “If there were 20 and now there are 13, did more or fewer than 10 go missing?” Counting from 13 to 20 on your fingers also works.
Most common mistake: answering 33, when your child added instead of subtracting. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Stage 4 — When they went missing · 3:10 (letter A)
How to think: The second batch came out at 3:35. To find out when it went into the oven, you have to go back 25 minutes. Calculation: 3:35 minus 25 minutes = 3:10.
If they get stuck, ask: “If the batch came out at 3:35, did it go into the oven before or after that?” Use an analog clock and go back 25 minutes.
Most common mistake: answering 4:00, when your child added the 25 minutes instead of going back in time. That answer leads to a different letter, and the word doesn't work out.
Code word: NANA. The person in the kitchen at 3:10 p.m. was NANA. She took 7 cookies to taste and to bring to the neighbor. Mystery solved, and nobody's in trouble!
